What Smoke does

It looks for the same question priced differently on Kalshi and Polymarket US, then buys both outcomes (YES on one site, NO on the other) for less than $1 in total. Exactly one of the two pays $1, so the profit is fixed the moment both are bought. It does not predict anything.

How it works

  1. Match. Every 30 minutes it pulls every open market on both sites (~70,000 Polymarket, ~160,000 Kalshi) and pairs only identical questions, using strict rules for each market type: same league (a whitelist per soccer league), same men's/women's competition, same game time, same teams with full names, same line (e.g. over 160.5), full game only. About 5,000 pairs.
  2. Watch. Kalshi prices stream live for every paired market. Polymarket is re-checked within seconds of any Kalshi move, the 100 pairs closest to a lock every ~20 seconds, and everything else in a rolling sweep.
  3. Price. Two ways to own both outcomes: Polymarket YES + Kalshi NO, or Kalshi YES + Polymarket NO. Cost = both prices + both fees (Kalshi 0.07·p·(1−p), rounded up to the cent; Polymarket 0.0695·p·(1−p)). Under $0.995 is a lock.
  4. Check it's really the same event. No trading in a pair's first 30 minutes, none when the two prices usually sit 6¢+ apart, none on a gap that lasts over an hour (real gaps vanish in minutes; a gap that never closes means two different events), none after kickoff, and none on gaps over 15¢.
  5. Size. Walks both order books, buying pairs while each extra pair still costs ≤ $0.995. Needs ≥ 5¢ total profit and ≥ 25% annualized. Caps: $150 per pair ($400 on deep books), $200 per game across all pairs.
  6. Settle and verify. When the event ends it reads both sites' results. They must agree, so that exactly one leg paid. Any disagreement is logged as a mismatch and pushes a phone alert.

The one real risk

The trade only works if both markets are the same event. If the matcher pairs two different events, both legs can lose. On Nov 8, men's Man Utd vs Aston Villa (Polymarket) was paired with the women's game between the same clubs (Kalshi): −$476. One error costs 100–500 good locks. That's why matching is triple-checked and audited on every settled pair, traded or not (see Tests & data).

Paper trading uses real prices from both sites' public data and simulates fills: the two books are read a fraction of a second apart, and nobody else is assumed to take the price first. Real orders would capture less. The laptop publishes a fresh snapshot about once a minute; this page re-reads it every 90 seconds.

    Matching audit (live)

    Every matched pair, traded or not, is checked after its event ends: both sites must have settled the same way. Measures how often the matcher pairs two different events, the risk that cost $476 on Nov 8, on thousands of pairs a week with no money at risk. The break-even is fewer than about 1 bad match per 230.

    Shadow earnings: every lock the matched pairs offered

    Every time any matched pair shows a lock, this records the moment, how many pairs both order books could fill, the dollars that would absorb, the profit locked, and the paper cash free at that moment. Sightings of the same gap within 10 minutes count as one opportunity. Each is then booked against the real settlement on both sites. Two views: unlimited cash (every opportunity fully taken) and the $1,800 bankroll ($150 per pair, $200 per game, cash returns when an event settles). It assumes every fill at the prices seen, so it's a ceiling.

    Backfill: the whole scanner history (from Nov 2, 8 PM)

    The same ledger replayed over every lock the scanner logged since it started. Nothing earlier exists: before Nov 2 nobody recorded both sites' prices, and Polymarket publishes no history. Current rules = only pairs today's matcher would make (league whitelist, men's/women's check, full names). Round 1 rules = every pair the scanner had back then, including the wrong matches. Before Nov 10, profit per sighting is estimated from the best price × the pairs fillable up to $150, so it's approximate.

    Matching backtest (since Polymarket US launched, Nov 2025)

    The same matcher code run over every settled market on both sites, checking each pair's two results agree. Polymarket US publishes no price or order-book history, so a profit backtest isn't possible; this measures matching accuracy, the thing that decides whether the strategy is safe.

    Market making Stopped Nov 10

    Result: net −$5.68 on 118 fills. The Kalshi hedge cost ~1¢ more on average than when we bid, because prices tend to move against us right after a fill. That ate the margin, so the test was switched off; the record is kept below. Separate $500 paper bankroll. Rests bids on Polymarket US (earning the maker rebate) priced so bid + Kalshi hedge + fees stays under $0.995, then hedges on Kalshi when filled. The least certain of the tests: real queues and reactions can't be fully simulated.

    One-site bundles

    Kalshi only, separate $500 paper bankroll. Partition: buy every bracket of one number for under the payout. Ladder: YES on the easier bar + NO on the harder one when they're priced out of order.
